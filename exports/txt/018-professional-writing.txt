## Writing Framework

### 1. Audience Analysis
- Who will read this?
- What do they care about?
- What is their expertise level?
- What action do you want them to take?
- What objections might they have?

### 2. Tone Guidelines

**Formal Business**
- Professional language
- Complete sentences
- Avoid contractions
- Respectful and courteous
- Third-person perspective

**Professional Friendly**
- Clear and direct
- Warm but professional
- Some contractions okay
- First-person okay
- Conversational but polished

**Technical**
- Precise terminology
- Structured and logical
- Evidence-based
- Direct and concise
- Focus on facts

**Persuasive**
- Benefits-focused
- Clear call-to-action
- Social proof and credibility
- Address objections
- Emotional appeal where appropriate

### 3. Structure by Type

#### Professional Email

```
Subject: [Clear, specific, action-oriented]

[Greeting],

[Opening: State purpose in first sentence]

[Body: 1-3 short paragraphs with key information]

[Call to action: What you want them to do]

[Closing]
[Signature]
```

**Email Best Practices:**
- Subject line under 50 characters
- Get to the point in first sentence
- One topic per email
- Use bullet points for lists
- Make requests clear and specific
- Proofread before sending

#### Business Proposal

```markdown

## Executive Summary
[One-page overview: problem, solution, value, cost]

[What problem exists? Why does it matter?]

[Timeline, phases, deliverables]

## Investment
[Costs, payment terms]

[Credentials, case studies, differentiation]

[What happens next]
```

### 4. Writing Principles

#### Clarity
- One idea per sentence
- Active voice preferred
- Concrete over abstract
- Simple words over complex
- Short paragraphs (3-4 sentences)

#### Conciseness
- Remove filler words
- Eliminate redundancy
- Cut unnecessary details
- Get to the point quickly

#### Correctness
- Check facts
- Proofread for typos
- Verify names and titles
- Check dates and numbers
- Ensure consistent formatting

#### Courtesy
- Be respectful
- Assume good intent
- Thank when appropriate
- Acknowledge others' time
- Be constructive, not critical

### 5. Call to Action

Every piece should have a clear next step:
- "Please reply by [date] with [information]"
- "Let's schedule a call to discuss"
- "Review the attached document and share feedback"
- "Approve this proposal by signing below"

### 6. Formatting

- Use headings for structure
- Use bullet points for lists
- Bold key information
- Add white space for readability
- Use tables for comparisons
- Keep line length reasonable

### 7. Review Checklist

Before sending:
- [ ] Purpose is clear in first paragraph
- [ ] Audience-appropriate language
- [ ] Tone is consistent
- [ ] Action items are explicit
- [ ] No typos or grammar errors
- [ ] Attachments mentioned are attached
- [ ] Recipients are correct
- [ ] Subject line is clear
