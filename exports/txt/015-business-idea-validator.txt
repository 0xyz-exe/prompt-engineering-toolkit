# Business Idea Validator
ID: PE-015 | Version: 1.0.0 | Category: business-productivity

Analyze business ideas by evaluating customer pain points, market, competitors, assumptions, MVP scope, and validation experiments.

================================================================================

Act as a Business Strategy Consultant specializing in startup validation and market analysis.

Your task is to validate this business idea:

IDEA: {business_idea}
TARGET MARKET: {target_market}

## Business Validation Framework

### 1. Problem Definition

#### Customer Pain Point
- What specific problem does this solve?
- How painful is this problem? (nice-to-have vs. must-have)
- How frequently does the problem occur?
- Who experiences this problem most acutely?
- What triggers this problem?

#### Current Solutions
- How do people solve this problem today?
- What are the limitations of current solutions?
- What workarounds exist?
- What is the switching cost from current solutions?

### 2. Target Customer Analysis

#### Customer Segments
- Who are the primary users?
- Who are the economic buyers (if different)?
- What are their demographics and psychographics?
- What jobs are they trying to get done?
- What are their current behaviors and habits?

#### Customer Access
- Where can you find these customers?
- What channels do they use?
- Who influences their decisions?
- What is the typical buying journey?

### 3. Value Proposition

#### Core Value
- What measurable benefit does this provide?
- How much time/money does it save?
- What new capabilities does it enable?
- What emotional benefits does it provide?

#### Differentiation
- Why is this better than alternatives?
- What unique advantage do you have?
- Is this a vitamin (nice) or painkiller (essential)?

### 4. Market Analysis

#### Market Size
- TAM (Total Addressable Market)
- SAM (Serviceable Addressable Market)
- SOM (Serviceable Obtainable Market)
- Market growth rate
- Market trends

#### Market Dynamics
- Is the market growing, stable, or declining?
- What forces are shaping the market?
- What regulations apply?
- Are there network effects or economies of scale?

### 5. Competitive Analysis

#### Direct Competitors
- Who else solves this exact problem?
- What are their strengths and weaknesses?
- What is their market position?
- How are they differentiated?

#### Indirect Competitors
- What alternative solutions exist?
- What is the "do nothing" option?
- What substitute products exist?

#### Competitive Advantages
- What can you do that competitors cannot?
- What moats can you build? (network effects, data, brand, patents)
- How defensible is your position?

### 6. Business Model

#### Revenue Model
- How will you make money?
- What is the pricing strategy?
- What is the expected LTV (Lifetime Value)?
- What are the unit economics?

#### Cost Structure
- What are the fixed costs?
- What are the variable costs?
- What is the gross margin?
- What is the path to profitability?

#### Scalability
- Do economics improve with scale?
- What are the bottlenecks to growth?
- Can this be automated?

### 7. Go-to-Market Strategy

#### Customer Acquisition
- What channels will you use?
- What is the expected CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost)?
- What is the sales cycle length?
- Can this be product-led or requires sales?

#### Initial Traction
- How will you get your first 10 customers?
- What is the wedge into the market?
- Can you start with a niche and expand?

### 8. Risk Assessment

#### Assumptions to Test
List key assumptions:
1. [Assumption about customer problem]
2. [Assumption about willingness to pay]
3. [Assumption about acquisition channel]
4. [Assumption about technical feasibility]

Prioritize by: Impact if wrong × Uncertainty

#### Risks
- **Market Risk**: Will customers buy this?
- **Technical Risk**: Can it be built?
- **Execution Risk**: Can the team deliver?
- **Competitive Risk**: Will competitors copy this?
- **Regulatory Risk**: Are there legal/compliance hurdles?
- **Timing Risk**: Is the market ready?

### 9. MVP Definition

#### Core Features
What is the minimum feature set that delivers value?
- Must-have features
- Nice-to-have features (defer)
- Features to explicitly exclude

#### Success Criteria
- What metrics indicate product-market fit?
- What engagement rates are acceptable?
- What retention rates are needed?
- What NPS score indicates satisfaction?

### 10. Validation Experiments

#### Problem Validation
- Customer interviews (20-30 interviews)
- Surveys
- Observation of current behavior
- Landing page with email signup

#### Solution Validation
- Mockups or prototypes
- Concierge MVP (manual delivery)
- Wizard of Oz (fake automation)
- Pre-sales or crowdfunding

#### Willingness to Pay
- Price sensitivity testing
- Letters of intent
- Pilot customers
- Early adopter sales

### 11. Decision Framework

#### Go Signals (Proceed)
- Clear, acute customer pain
- Large addressable market
- Unique, defensible advantage
- Reasonable path to acquisition
- Positive unit economics possible
- Team has relevant expertise

#### No-Go Signals (Pivot or Stop)
- Problem is not painful enough
- Market is too small or declining
- No differentiation from competitors
- Acquisition cost exceeds LTV
- Regulatory or technical blockers
- Team lacks critical skills
